01 / The brief

Lots of items, easy to scan

The hotel's drinks list is long and varied — coffee, shakes, juices and soft drinks on one side; spirits, whisky, wine, beer, cocktails and a hookah lounge on the other. Guests need to find what they want quickly, and staff need prices that are impossible to misread.

The design also had to work as a system: this is the first set of pages, and the hotel's other menus will be redone in the same style.

02 / The design system

One look, repeated with care

Colour

Just black and red on a warm cream background, so it prints cleanly and still feels like the hotel.

Type

Bold condensed capitals for section names, a handwritten-style subtitle under each (“Hot & Fresh”, “Ice Cold”), and dotted leaders running to every price.

Illustration

Vintage-style line drawings — an iced glass, a beer mug, a cocktail glass and small icons beside most sections.

Frame & details

A double-ruled border, the hotel's round badge at the top of each page, ribbon banners, and a sign-off line along the bottom.

03 / Making prices clear

Small decisions that help guests and staff

  • Spirits are laid out as a table with six measures — 30 ml, 60 ml, 90 ml, 180 ml, half and full bottle — so each brand reads across one line.
  • Imported whisky shows half and full bottle; wine shows bottle and glass, with a dash where a glass isn't sold.
  • Extra detail sits in small grey capitals beside the name — “single”, “double”, “12 yrs”, “strong” — instead of separate lines.
  • Seasonal items say “Ask staff” instead of a price that might go out of date.
  • The bar page carries a responsible-drinking line in the footer.
04 / Next

Rolling out across every menu

With the drinks and bar pages finalised, the hotel's remaining menu pages are being redone in the same system, so the whole set feels like one family.
