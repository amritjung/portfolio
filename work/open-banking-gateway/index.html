01 / The challenge

One payment flow, many different banks

Open Banking lets a customer pay a business directly from their bank account, without a card. The payment is started through each bank's payment initiation (PISP) API and the customer approves it inside their own banking app or website.

The catch is that every bank implements the standard in its own way, and every payment has to meet strict rules: PSD2 regulation, Strong Customer Authentication, and GDPR for the customer data passing through. The goal was a single, reliable gateway that hides all of that behind one consistent payment flow.

02 / My role

Architect, developer and the bank's technical contact

  • Architected and built the MVP of the gateway, integrated with 18+ UK banks via their PISP APIs.
  • Wrote the first version in vanilla PHP, then migrated the whole codebase to Laravel.
  • Acted as the technical contact for each bank's team — validating integrations and resolving compatibility issues.
  • Implemented OAuth 2.0 and Strong Customer Authentication to meet PSD2 requirements.
  • Built the full payment lifecycle: request handling, real-time status monitoring, webhook processing and automated customer notifications.
  • Designed the customer-facing interface in Blade and JavaScript for payment authorisation, consent management and transaction history.
03 / The rebuild

From vanilla PHP to Laravel

The gateway started life as a vanilla PHP application. Once that version was working, I moved everything across to Laravel — the bank integrations, the payment lifecycle, the OAuth 2.0 and SCA handling, and the customer-facing interface — so the MVP ended up on a single, consistent framework.

04 / How a payment flows

From “Pay by bank” to money received

  • Customer
  • Business
  • Gateway (my build)
  • Customer's bank
  1. 1

    Customer → Business

    Chooses “Pay by bank”

    At checkout, the customer picks their bank instead of entering card details.

  2. 2

    Business → Gateway

    Payment request created

    The gateway receives the request, validates it and records it before anything is sent to a bank.

  3. 3

    Gateway → Bank

    Payment initiated with the bank

    Using OAuth 2.0, the gateway sets up the payment through that bank's PISP API.

  4. 4

    Customer ↔ Bank

    Customer approves with SCA

    The customer is sent to their own bank, logs in and approves the payment with Strong Customer Authentication.

  5. 5

    Bank → Gateway

    Back to the gateway

    The bank returns the customer, and the gateway picks up the result and carries the payment forward.

  6. 6

    Gateway ↔ Bank

    Status tracked in real time

    The gateway keeps watching the payment until it reaches a final state.

  7. 7

    Gateway → Business + Customer

    Everyone is told

    Webhooks update the business's system and automated notifications go out to the customer.

06 / Security & compliance

Built for regulated money movement

PSD2

Designed around the PSD2 rules for payment initiation from the start, not added at the end.

OAuth 2.0 + SCA

Bank access through OAuth 2.0, with every payment approved by the customer using Strong Customer Authentication.

GDPR

End-to-end data encryption and two-factor authentication to protect customer data.

Audit trail

Detailed audit and error logging, so every payment and problem can be traced afterwards.

07 / How we worked

Testing the integration with each bank

For each bank, I worked directly with its technical team to validate the integration and resolve any compatibility issues. API changes were tested in Postman, and bugs raised during QA were fixed as part of the sprint.

Day to day, I took part in daily stand-ups and sprint planning with the team, working remotely for a London-based company.

08 / Outcome

A working MVP across 18+ banks

  • Completed a working MVP of the gateway, integrated with 18+ UK banks.
  • PSD2 and Strong Customer Authentication requirements designed in from the ground up, not bolted on.
  • Started as a freelance engagement in November 2020 and was converted to a full-time role in December 2021.

Before a payments product like this can launch in the UK, the company needs approval from the Financial Conduct Authority (FCA). With the MVP complete, the next step was waiting on that authorisation — a long process that was slowed further by COVID.

Details are kept high-level to respect the confidentiality of the company and its partner banks.
